# Predicting which customers will leave

A churn model built for a Kaggle competition on telecom customer data. The question: looking at a customer's contract, services and billing, can we tell in advance who is likely to cancel?

The data is synthetic (generated by Kaggle from a real telecom dataset), with almost 600,000 customers, nearly 1 in 4 of whom left.

## The result

The model ranks customers by how likely they are to leave. In plain terms: take one customer who left and one who stayed, and the model gives the higher risk score to the one who left 92 times out of 100 (a score of 0.917, the measure used in the competition).

## What drives churn

These patterns came out of the analysis before any modelling, and each one is something a CRM team can act on.

| What we looked at | Who leaves most | Who stays |
|---|---|---|
| Contract | Monthly contracts: 42% left | Two-year contracts: 1% left |
| Payment | Paying by electronic check: 49% left | Automatic payment by card or bank: 7 to 8% left |
| How long they've been a customer | First 6 months: 54% left | Over 4 years: 5% left |
| Support services | No tech support: 40% left | With tech support: 10% left |
| Internet type | Fiber: 42% left | No internet plan: 1% left |

## What this means for CRM

The analysis shows two profiles side by side: what a customer who leaves looks like, and what a customer who stays active looks like.

- **The customer who stays** has a yearly or two-year contract, pays automatically, uses support services, and has been with the company for a while.
- **The customer at risk** is new, on a monthly plan, pays manually, and has no support services.

That gives CRM a clear goal. Instead of reacting once someone cancels, each journey moves an at-risk customer one step closer to the profile of a customer who stays.

## Pre-emptive journeys

| Risk signal | Journey | Goal: one step closer to staying |
|---|---|---|
| New customer, first 6 months | Onboarding that shows the value of the plan and where to get help | Reach month 6 |
| Monthly contract | Upgrade offer to a yearly plan | Switch to a longer contract |
| Pays manually | Nudge to set up automatic payment | Switch to automatic payment |
| No support services | Trial of tech support or online security | Add a support service |
| Risk score rises | Personal check-in or retention offer | Stay active before they cancel |

In a CRM tool like Braze, the model's risk score can be added to each customer profile and refreshed regularly. The journeys then start on their own when a customer shows a risk signal, not after they have already left.

One caution: these patterns show who tends to leave, not why. A journey proves it works only when it is tested against a control group that doesn't receive it.

## With real activity data

This dataset only shows what each customer has (contract, payment, services), not what they do. In a real CRM setup, behaviour is usually the stronger signal, and the same idea goes further:

1. **Learn the rhythm of the best customers.** For example, they buy or sell every few weeks, or log in at least once a month.
2. **Watch for a missed window.** When a customer goes longer than their usual rhythm without a transaction or action, that is the early warning, often well before they would cancel.
3. **Start a journey at that moment.** A reminder, a relevant offer or a reason to come back, timed to the gap rather than to a fixed calendar.
4. **Measure it.** Compare the customers who got the journey with a control group that didn't, and keep only what brings people back.

Account traits like the ones above then add a second layer: they show who needs that attention most.

## How it was built

For readers who want the detail:

- **Exploration** (`notebook/churn_eda.ipynb`): churn rates by every customer trait, and which traits matter most.
- **Preparation** (`notebook/churn_preprocessing_feature_engineering.ipynb`): cleaning and new features.
- **Modelling** (`notebook/churn_modelling*.ipynb`, `notebook/churn_ensemble_v9.ipynb`): three models (LightGBM, XGBoost and CatBoost), each checked on five separate slices of the data, then combined into one final score.
- **Submissions** (`submissions/`): the files sent to Kaggle as the model improved.
